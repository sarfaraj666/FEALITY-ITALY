FAQs

Stainless steel kitchens

1. What steel does FEALITY ITALY use for its kitchens?

Our kitchens use 304-grade stainless steel. When comparing designs, ask the team to identify the cabinet components and finishes included in your quotation. For example, ask how the cabinet body, shutter and shelf are each specified. This gives you a clearer picture of what you are choosing.

2. Why choose stainless steel around the sink?

Steel resists moisture and does not swell like wood. An accessible plumbing connection still matters so that a leak can be spotted and repaired. When planning the sink unit, leave room to reach the shut-off valve even with bins and cleaning supplies inside.

3. Can termites damage stainless steel cabinetry?

Termites cannot feed on steel. If your design includes wood-based panels or trims, ask about those components separately. If you are replacing a wooden kitchen, check the surrounding skirting and door frames too. These are separate from the new cabinet body.

4. How can I plan a kitchen for Indian cooking?

Start with your largest kadai, pressure cooker and tawa. Plan drawers that fit them, a landing space beside the hob and a convenient place for the spices you use daily. Think through a meal you cook often, from washing vegetables to serving rotis. That routine can help you decide where prep space will be most useful.

5. Does a steel kitchen have to look metallic?

No. Explore coloured fronts, glass panels and other finishes in the collection. Ask to see a sample of your preferred finish in the light of your own kitchen. Hold the sample beside your countertop and floor in both daylight and evening light. The same colour can look quite different under the two.

6. How should I clean my cabinet fronts?

Use a soft cloth and the care instructions for your particular finish. Dry water spots after cleaning and check before using abrasive pads or strong cleaners. For handles and grooves, wipe along the edges where residue collects. Use a clean second cloth to dry the surface.

Kitchen layout and storage

7. Which measurements should I take first?

Measure each wall, ceiling height and door and window positions. Mark the sink drain, water inlet and electrical outlets on the same sketch. Add a photo of each wall and label it on the sketch. A column, low beam or projecting window sill is much easier to understand this way.

8. Will an island fit in my kitchen?

Mark its footprint on the floor with tape. Open the fridge, oven and nearby drawers, then walk around them as you would while cooking. If you plan to add stools, test the space with someone seated while another person carries a plate past them.

9. Can I keep my existing appliances?

Share each appliance model and its installation guide. The designer can check opening sizes, ventilation and door clearance before finalising the layout. For example, a fridge may fit the opening but still need its door to swing wider to pull out a vegetable drawer.

10. How do I plan corner storage?

List what you want to keep in the corner, then compare how easily you can reach those items with shelves, pull-outs or drawers on the adjoining run. Try reaching for your heaviest pot in the proposed layout. Check whether you would need to lift another item out first.

11. Where should I store everyday cookware?

Keep your most-used pans near the hob and plates near the serving area. Measure tall bottles and stacked pots before choosing drawer depths. Consider a separate slot for lids or chopping boards so that taking out one pan does not disturb the whole stack.

Wardrobes, vanities and balcony cabinets

12. How much hanging space should my wardrobe have?

Measure your longest hanging garment and the rail length your clothes occupy today. Use those figures to divide space between long hanging, short hanging and drawers. Include sarees, coats or dresses you hang only occasionally. One full-height bay can sit alongside shorter rails for shirts and trousers.

13. Should I choose open or closed wardrobe storage?

Open shelves keep everyday pieces visible. Closed fronts conceal occasional-use items and help keep dust off clothing. A mix works well when you use the same wardrobe for both. For example, an open shelf at eye level can hold tomorrow's outfit while closed drawers keep smaller accessories together.

14. What should I measure for a bathroom vanity?

Record the wall width, basin height and pipe positions. Check that the bathroom door and vanity drawers can open at the same time. Also measure how far the trap projects from the wall. Its position affects the usable depth of the top drawer.

15. How do I choose a vanity mirror size?

Consider the basin width, the eye heights of the people using it and any nearby lights or shelves. Mark the mirror outline on the wall to check the view. Check the outline from your usual standing position. If the mirror has a cabinet behind it, allow room for its door to swing past the tap.

16. Can a balcony cabinet accommodate my washing machine?

Several Balcony designs show integrated laundry storage. Share the appliance dimensions, hose positions and service clearances so the layout can be checked for your machine. Check how you load the machine, too. A front-loader needs door clearance, while a top-loader needs free space above the lid.

17. Is a balcony cabinet suitable for an exposed balcony?

Tell the team whether the area receives direct rain or sun. Ask them to confirm the suitability of the proposed finish and hardware for that exposure before choosing a design. A photo taken just after rain can help show where water reaches the cabinet wall or collects on the floor.

Choosing and planning a design

18. Where can I see the full collections?

Open Kitchen, Wardrobes, Vanity or Balcony from the navigation. Select a photograph to inspect the design in the full-screen viewer. Use the viewer arrows to compare neighbouring designs. On a phone, swipe between photographs to see another layout or storage detail.

19. How can I ask about a specific photograph?

Include the collection and design name in your enquiry. If two photographs share a name, also mention the photo number shown in the viewer. A screenshot is useful if you are asking about one feature, such as an illuminated shelf or the drawer beneath a basin.

20. Can I adapt a gallery design for a smaller room?

Yes, you can use a gallery photograph as a starting point. Tell the designer which features you want to keep and share the room measurements to explore what fits. For example, if the island in a kitchen photo is too large, ask how its preparation space or drawer storage could fit along a wall.

21. What should I bring to a consultation?

A rough floor plan, a few room photos and a list of appliances or items you need to store are useful. Include your preferred finishes and your budget range if known. Mention one thing that frustrates you about your current room. A hard-to-reach shelf can tell the designer more than a list of finishes.

22. What happens between consultation and installation?

The project moves through consultation, design, confirmation, production and installation. Review the layout and finish selections with the team at confirmation. Before approving the drawing, walk through door swings, drawer positions and appliance access. This is a useful time to flag a change.

23. Can I coordinate finishes across rooms?

Bring your shortlisted kitchen, wardrobe and vanity designs to the same consultation. Compare finish samples together and confirm which selections are available for each room. Place the samples beside a finish the rooms already share, such as flooring. This helps you judge how they will look when viewed through an open doorway.

Quotes, installation and warranty

24. Are the gallery photographs a price quotation?

A photograph shows a design idea. Request a written quotation for your room dimensions, chosen finish, fittings and installation scope. If a photograph includes decorative lighting or appliances, identify the items you want priced so you know what the figure covers.

25. How can I compare two quotations fairly?

Compare cabinet sizes, materials, hardware and included services line by line. Check whether appliances, delivery and installation appear in each total. For instance, two units of equal width may contain different numbers of drawers. Mark that difference before comparing their prices.

26. How long will my project take?

Ask for a project-specific schedule after the design and site requirements are confirmed. Check the expected production, delivery and installation dates separately. If you are coordinating flooring or painting, tell the team when those trades finish so the installation date can be planned around site readiness.

27. How should I prepare the room for installation?

Confirm access, working space and the required plumbing and electrical points with the installation team before delivery. Share any building access restrictions in advance. Check lift dimensions, delivery hours and the route from the building entrance to the room. Flag tight corners before the cabinets arrive.

28. Which FEALITY ITALY products carry a lifetime warranty?

Every FEALITY ITALY product comes with a lifetime warranty. Ask for the written coverage for your selected product, including covered components, care requirements and exclusions. If your design includes appliances or accessories, ask which warranty document applies to each item and whom to contact for service.

29. What information should I keep for a warranty enquiry?

Keep your invoice, design confirmation and warranty paperwork together. When contacting the team, include the product name, a photo of the issue and a short description. For a drawer or door issue, include one wider photo identifying the cabinet and a close-up of the affected fitting.
